### Abstract: When teachers assign group work, students are expected to work together on a provided problem. What teachers may not notice is how they participate and collaborate, and how they affect a group’s progress and under- standing. The paper investigates how students collaborate and share ideas for finding patterns and transformations of generalized magic squares by using the basic 3-by-3 magic square as the base solution. The study uses student-used digital interfaces like Google Slides and Google Drawings, video recordings, and multimodal student responses to conjecture about the effect of group discussion and participation, or the lack thereof, on student reasoning and conjecture. The study’s results demonstrate a noticeable difference in active cooperation and co-construction regarding students’ reasoning, favoring co-construction and both the sharing and revision of ideas over mathematical maturity. These findings reinforce the findings of Barron (2003); both conceptual and social cooperation are necessary to tackle a difficult and wide-ranging problem regardless of mathematical maturity.
